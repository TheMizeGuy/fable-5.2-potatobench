## The list, and what was done

Fixed in the build after this round
1. "The game keeps pausing itself" (all three). It did not: a press on the pause key or Space 0.5 to 1 s after an
   answer, a paper put away or the guide had already set the days going paused them again (x1: 023, 025, 052, 055;
   x3: 014, 050). One such press within 2 s is now read as "go on", on Space, the clock's key and the Running chip.
2. A right-drag begun between the two boards did nothing, so a view tipped straight down could not be tipped back
   (x3: 041, 042). The top bar's own box, the width of the window and as tall as its tallest board, was taking the
   press: a stylesheet rule of the same weight had turned it back on two days before. Presses there reach the land
   again, and so do presses beside the goal slip in its row.
3. One Plant stamp gives the word for every field and the other stamps go; the next presses of the run opened a
   field's card (x2: 042) and the yard's, which then came up from behind a telegram "by itself" (x1: 044 to 046).
   For a moment after the word, a press on the land is taken by nothing.
4. The yard's key for selling logs printed its name one word a line over its price (x1: 046).
5. A click on Sell while the scale was still weighing missed: the ticket was laid out taller until the scale was
   done, and its keys then moved up 123 px (x3: 027, 028). The keys now stand where they will stay.
6. The second spring's guide ran ahead and then back (x1: 042; x2: 032, 033, 037): "Got it" on a note that asks for a
   click, then the click, left the next step's note up over the field's record or the People fold. A note about what
   was just opened now comes in front, and the step it interrupted comes back after.
7. "Your first year" over a note met in year two and "Your second spring" in year three (x2: 053, 055): a note met
   late is headed with the year it is met in.
8. "Choose potatoes or a rest year for Home Woodlot" over a card that offers pulling stumps, on a plan that opened
   with last year's woodlot still in hand (x2: 055). A new spring opens with nothing in hand, and the crop note
   keeps to fields that can be planted.
9. "Fields dry, Brook Field wors..." with its amount cut off (x3: 047). The amount is never the part cut; the words
   are shorter ("Brook Field driest"). To do names a field drying out through the whole of bulking, as the board does.
10. "Brook Field looks heavier: +$1,000" after Brook Field was sold (x2: 027): a crop that is in "brought more than
