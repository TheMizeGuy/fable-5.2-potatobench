# Round three: the build with round two's fixes

Three fresh blind players (the same three characters, 55 requests and about ten minutes each, pictures only) played
main at 75c5aa4: the routine, the people card, the guide's second version, the slip and money work, fixed order
places, tags held to their fields. Their notes and the record of every request are beside this file
(`round-3-r*-notes.md`, `w3-r*.jsonl`).

## Scores, all three rounds (out of ten)

| Player | Knowing what to do next | Looks | Wanting to keep playing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strategy veteran (took the guide in rounds two and three) | 6, 5, 5 | 9, 9, 9 | 7, 7, 7 |
| Careful casual (took the guide in rounds two and three) | 6, 6, 7 | 9, 9, 9 | 8, 7, 8 |
| Impatient explorer (turned the guide down) | 3, 4, 4 | 9, 9, 8 | 6, 6, 6 |

The interface is not yet "much more intuitive" by this measure. One player of three moved up a point; the others did
not move. What changed is what they complain about.

## What no longer came up

- People standing idle with work due (round one's worst).
- Clicks landing on the wrong thing because the camera moved or a card's rows shifted (rounds one and two's worst):
  not reported by any of the three. One player's "Show me" click landed on something else once (r1: 018) and the
  spring sheet's folds still shift rows when one closes (r1: 050).
- The mouse "going dead" after a letter key (it was tags spreading when a card closed).
- A hand worked into an injury; orders vanishing unexplained; a goal that cannot be paid for.
- Nobody was lost at the start: all three were in a running season within a few requests, and the two who took the
  guide could do nearly every step it asked.

## What they found

1. **The red "cannot wait" cards** (all three; new in this build): stacked at the left, squeezed to one word a
   line, lying over the spraying question and the test-dig card, and seeming to do nothing when pressed because the
   next field's card took the place at once (r1: 020 to 033; r2: 019, 020; r3: 013 to 022, 054). **Fixed after they
   played**: one row, at its natural width.
2. **Spraying is still the hard part.** One player's lone farmer never sprayed all year: the cards said nobody would
   take it "as things stand" and nothing said what to change; the hiring sheet in year two was the first place that
   said spraying is tractor work (r1: 019 to 034, 053). Another chose "keep the fields sprayed" and was asked to spray
   again (r2: 022, 026). The cards came back every few weeks (r3). The guide's wording is corrected since; the cause
   of the lone farmer refusing the work is not found.
3. **The game blames the player for what it asked.** The guide or the slip asked for the vines to be killed; the
   weigh ticket then listed "Too small: the vines were killed on Sep 8, before these sized" (r2: 024, 028), and for
   one player "41 days before they were due", after blight had forced it (r1: 030, 032).
4. **The crew acts without saying so**: planting happened unseen (r2: 010 to 012, 052); a field was dug "with the
   vines still green" at a cost of 264 cwt (r3: 033); players could not tell which jobs were theirs to give (r3).
5. **The guide's numbering jumps** ("2 of 12", then "10 of 12", then "9 of 12") and lessons pass unseen while the
   days run (r1, r2); its box lay over the equipment list (r1: 049); a note of its held the clock and swallowed a 3x
   click (r2: 049); one step is worded for the keyboard only (r1: 055).
6. **A click on the player's own portrait** opens no card, only a "You" tag over the farmer (r1: 027; r2: 016, 017).
   A click on a field's tag sometimes gave only an outline (r3: 019, 028).
7. **Storing against selling**: "Store it: brings most" and the month's forecast were followed and paid less than
   selling at the dig (r1: 044, 045; r3: 031 to 044); for one player storing paid (r2: 042).
8. **"Name a foreman"** still could not be done by one player from what the screen showed (r3: 045 to 048).
9. Still unexplained on screen: why the loan moves between days, the year's number against the ledger's, what
   the "First" icons on a person's card do, hold back seed, "pick the new ground".
10. Zoomed in, people are face badges; a tractor can be seen, a person on foot was not (r3: 023, 024).

What they liked: the weigh ticket; the test-dig drawings; a person's card ("exactly what I expect from an RTS");
a right-click that answers with a reason; the order grid with its keys and greyed orders; the balance turning from
red to black; beating the neighbour; the land in every season.
