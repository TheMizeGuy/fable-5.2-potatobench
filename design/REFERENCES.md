# References: what the papers were learned from

Each source was downloaded and looked at full size. The scans live outside the repository; this file keeps the
address and the one thing taken from each.

## Farm paper and print

| Source | Address | What I took |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Post Office Savings Bank deposit book, inside pages (1869-74) | https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Inside_a_Post_Office_Savings_Bank_deposit_book.jpg | The pass book for the money. Printed captions in small italic ("Name of Depositor", "Amount of Deposit"), heavy column rules with fine sub-columns for the units, the clerk's hand in the cells, and round dated office stamps in black and red overlapping the rules. That is where the violet date stamp on the cash comes from, and the rule that a printed caption is italic while an entry is roman. |
| W. & T. Avery weighing machine ticket (1929) | https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Avery_weighing_machine_ticket_-_1929-07-26_(obverse).jpg | The weigh ticket. A buff card with a ruled inner border, the heading in plain gothic capitals, a box divided into cells for the units, and the weight itself stamped in violet by the machine, a different ink and a different hand from the printing. Every stamped figure in the system (weights, the ticket number, today's date) is violet for this reason. |

## Games, studied as frames (Steam store screenshots, https://store.steampowered.com/app/<id>)

| Game | App | What it showed me |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pentiment | 1205520 | A whole interface made of the world's own documents, with a typeface for each kind of writer. The proof that paper can be the interface without becoming a scrapbook: its speech scrolls are flat, cleanly cut, and never stained or torn for effect. I held the papers to the same: clean edges, real cuts (a tag, a perforation, a coupon), no coffee rings, no fake age. |
| Frostpunk | 323190 | One instrument with ornament (the temperature dial) and everything else plain. Thin double rules as section heads. Workforce rows as a table of numbers with tiny controls on either side. The calendar pad is the one carried object of the season screen with a mount; the rest are flat sheets. |
| Disco Elysium | 632470 | Big numerals with a short label under them; paper-white strips cut hard against a painted world; lists in condensed capitals. The weigh ticket's net weight and the calendar's day are set this way. |
| Against the Storm | 1336490 | A dense strategy screen held together by one icon family drawn in one hand, every resource at the same size and weight. The 59 cuts here are drawn on one grid for the same reason. Also what to avoid: ornament on every frame. |
| Manor Lords | 1363080 | How little interface a painterly strategy scene needs, and how a light scene (snow) takes a light panel if the panel has a firm edge. The rule that every sheet carries a dark keyline came from looking at its snow. |
| Farthest Frontier, RimWorld, Citizen Sleeper | 1044720, 294100, 1578650 | RimWorld's work grid as the one table worth having; Citizen Sleeper's single clear verb per card; Farthest Frontier as the generic dark-panel look this direction is meant to leave behind. |

## The three that shaped it most

1. The savings bank pass book: printed italic captions, ruled cells, entries in another hand, round dated stamps.
2. The Avery weigh ticket: the machine's violet figures on a printed buff card.
3. The 1869 auction bill: condensed wood type, Oxford rules and the printer's imprint, the discipline of a jobbing shop.
